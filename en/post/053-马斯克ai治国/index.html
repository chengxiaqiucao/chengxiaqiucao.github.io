Musk's Audit Storm, an AI Shock More Direct Than DeepSeek

A wave of reform across the ocean, and in just over ten days a handful of genius engineers barely in their twenties uncovered so many audit problems. How did they do it?

Feb 10, 2025
6 minute read
中文

During the Spring Festival holiday, everyone here was celebrating in high spirits. The box office of Ne Zha kept climbing; DeepSeek also broke into the mainstream, dominating everyone’s feeds, with its servers busy for long stretches.

At the same time, across the ocean, as Trump moved into the White House on January 20, Musk also took charge of DOGE and got to work at lightning speed that very day, launching a large-scale audit of federal government spending.

This DOGE is not the cryptocurrency DOGE; it is short for the U.S. Department of Government Efficiency.

Musk and the DOGE Audit Storm

Unlike the traditional large-scale audits we might imagine — first setting up an audit department, then hundreds or even thousands of auditors spending months on preparation, on-site work, and reconciliation — DOGE has, in less than 20 days, already turned American politics upside down.

— January 20: shut down the Chief Diversity Officers Executive Council (CDOEC);

— January 25: canceled about $420 million in existing / soon-to-be-signed contracts;

— January 27: the Departments of Labor, Transportation, Agriculture, Commerce, Health and Human Services, and the Treasury canceled 16 DEIA contracts, saving $145 million;

— January 28: the General Services Administration (GSA) terminated three leases on nearly empty office space and shut down 17 DEIA websites, saving $1.6 million;

— January 29: canceled a $45 million DEI scholarship for Myanmar;

(As of January 29, 2025, 26 departments including the Department of Education and the GSA had terminated 85 DEIA-related contracts totaling about $1 billion;)

— February 3: the number of terminated leases on underutilized buildings rose to 22, and the savings grew from $1.6 million to $44.6 million;

— February 3: shut down the United States Agency for International Development (USAID);

— February 4: six agencies terminated 36 contracts, saving about $165 million in total, including the Department of Homeland Security’s “Humanities Research and Climate Support Services” contract;

— February 5: terminated 12 consulting contracts (at the GSA and the Department of Education), saving about $30 million in total;

— February 6: the Social Security Administration terminated its “Gender X Initiative Marker” contract, saving over $1 million; NASA canceled its Politico subscription, saving $500,000; 78 contracts in the DEI, distressed assets, media, and consulting categories were terminated, for total savings of about $110 million;

— February 8: announced that within the past 48 hours, the Department of Health and Human Services had canceled 62 contracts worth $182 million; the Department of Education terminated three DEI training grants totaling $15 million.

Yet behind such fast and efficient audit results and actions there is no massive team — it relies on nothing more than a few geniuses barely in their twenties, backed by their AI algorithms.

DOGE’s Six Young Prodigies

Hard to imagine, but every move on the list above was carried out by the six young men below.

  • Gavin Kliger (25): A graduate of UC Berkeley in electrical engineering and computer science, Kliger was formerly a senior software engineer at the U.S. AI data analytics company Databricks and is an expert in artificial intelligence and big data analysis. His job is to draft executive orders and ensure they take effect quickly within the government — the email telling all USAID staff they need not return to headquarters on Monday came from his hand. He also directly drafted the funding review order targeting the U.S. Treasury, took part in OPM’s mass layoff plan, and was responsible for setting the “rehire standards” for civil servants, requiring that future government employees be approved by DOGE before being hired.

  • Luke Farritor (23): Farritor dropped out of the University of Nebraska–Lincoln and later worked for Nat Friedman, the Silicon Valley entrepreneur behind GitHub. He once interned at SpaceX, writing software for the pumps, valves, and other components that help fuel rockets. Farritor is currently listed as an “executive engineer” in the office of the Secretary of Health and Human Services (HHS), and personally oversaw the digital lockdown operation at USAID; his next target is already the systems of the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services, which handles contracts.

  • Edward Coristine (19): The youngest of the group, Coristine had just graduated from high school and was studying mechanical engineering and physics at Northeastern University in Boston. He spent three months last summer working at Musk’s brain-computer interface company Neuralink, and his specific duties have not yet been made clear. Coristine once joined a call with U.S. General Services Administration employees using a non-government Google Gmail account, asking them to review code he had written and justify their own work — a move that sparked dissatisfaction among federal employees.

  • Akash Bobba (21): Bobba comes from UC Berkeley and was an investment engineering intern at Bridgewater Associates; he had also interned at Meta and at Palantir, the U.S. software services company founded by MAGA backer Peter Thiel. He specializes in artificial intelligence, data analysis, and financial modeling. He is responsible for designing algorithms to monitor the flow of government funds, leading a “real-time fiscal spending monitoring system” that allows federal “inefficient spending” to be frozen in real time without congressional review.

  • Ethan Shaotran (22): A senior computer science major at Harvard, Shaotran is the only one on the team with AI startup experience. The AI company he founded, Energize AI, received $100,000 in funding from OpenAI, and he once took second place in a hackathon held by Musk’s AI company xAI. Shaotran leads DOGE’s AI government initiative, whose goal is to replace the government’s traditional human decision-making with algorithms, automating most administrative approvals, budget evaluations, and even congressional reports.

  • Gautier Cole Killian (24): Breached the Treasury’s security systems and obtained access to the payment network. A graduate of McGill University, Killian is exceptionally strong in financial data processing and once worked as an engineer at Jump Trading, a firm specializing in high-frequency financial trading and algorithms. His main task is to help the DOGE team break through the Treasury’s security defenses, giving them access to the government payment network and letting them bypass security checks to read millions of transaction records directly. Killian is also one of the few young people granted senior White House access, and Musk places extremely high trust in him.

So these few engineers, barely out of college, after obtaining data access and in only about two weeks, used AI to bring to the surface all sorts of problems that had lain hidden beneath the iceberg for decades. You could say it truly was a case of speed being everything — the bureaucrats over in the U.S. probably hadn’t even come to their senses before finding their house had been raided.

Reflections

No matter where this reform storm ultimately takes Musk and his six young lieutenants, AI has already, in a jaw-dropping way, begun to sway the political situation across the ocean, and it is bound to deeply affect every aspect of our lives.

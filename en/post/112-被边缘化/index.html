Feeling Marginalized by Your Leader at Work? How to Adjust

Jul 20, 2025
5 minute read
中文

New to the workplace, I always feel marginalized by my leader, and my state of mind is crumbling — how should I adjust myself?

As the saying goes, nine out of ten things in life do not go as one wishes. Many people new to the workplace brim with ambition but have no chance to display it, and can easily feel overlooked or even deliberately marginalized, leading to an unbalanced mindset and endless frustration.

In this article we briefly discuss this topic.

Are You Really Being Marginalized?

For most people, from childhood through their school years, the path of action was in most cases clear: study hard and you get good grades; whatever you do, the result mostly matches expectations.

But once you enter the workplace and step into society, things are not quite the same. You work hard and act positively, yet the results may not match your expectations. Not only do you fail to earn your leader’s recognition — you may even get a cold splash of water — and it is easy to feel targeted and marginalized.

So, is this really being marginalized?

First of all, you should believe that the so-called marginalization is not a common phenomenon within a team.

People often tend to be self-centered, viewing the things around them from a first-person perspective and focusing on how others respond to their actions. They also tend to act according to their own fixed ways of thinking and understanding — full of enthusiasm, yet not necessarily met with applause.

In fact, if you shift your perspective and look from the leader’s or the team’s point of view, problems are often easy to spot. Is your “hard work and proactive behavior” truly valuable to the team?

Huawei once had a newly hired graduate student from Peking University who, right after joining, wrote a long, ten-thousand-word letter to Ren Zhengfei, discussing at length his suggestions for the company’s future development.

Ren Zhengfei replied — If this person is mentally ill, it is recommended that he be sent to a hospital for treatment; if he is not ill, it is recommended that he be dismissed

Looking at it from the team’s perspective again, in any team resources are always tight, and human resources are especially precious. In reality, no leader likes to put a capable person on the bench.

So in many cases, the so-called “being marginalized” is very likely just an illusion caused by your own sense of letdown. More often, once you find the crux of the problem and make the adjustment, you can move past it.

How to Adjust?

So how do you better adapt to the team and find your place?

It is really what was mentioned earlier — aligning your personal value and the team’s value in the same direction.

If the so-called marginalization is mostly because you have not yet delivered the value you should, then find the corresponding cause and make targeted improvements.

There may be various reasons for not delivering value:

  • Business capability — work hard to improve your professional skills and learn with an open mind
  • Collaboration — in teamwork you cannot be a lone wolf; maintain smooth cooperation with your teammates
  • Personal reasons — put simply, you overrate yourself and are ambitious but inexperienced; you think you deserve a certain position, but your actual ability and influence are not yet up to it

And to determine where the crux lies, besides your own observation and attempts, the more effective approach is direct communication with your leader.

Understand your leader’s (team’s) expectations of you, clarify what your position, the work you take on, and your effective output are, and then make targeted changes.

Really Being Marginalized

Of course, wherever there are people there is a “world” of its own, and we cannot rule out the possibility that you are genuinely considered not worth cultivating by your leader and really are marginalized.

But even so, there is no need to be too discouraged — there is still room.

Because, looking at the different situations of members within a team, being marginalized may not be the worst case.

Within a team, members can roughly be divided into the following types:

  • High-value employees can take charge of key work and core business, and enjoy various incentives tilted in their favor
  • Average-value employees mainly handle routine work, assist in the completion of core business, and receive the team’s normal incentives
  • Low-value employees, that is, “marginalized” employees, usually do peripheral work that does not affect the team’s development and rarely receive incentives
  • Negative-value employees are those awaiting elimination and need to be removed from the team as early as possible

And being marginalized, although fairly dangerous within a team, does not go so far as elimination; it can be said to be in a probationary period.

At this point, through direct communication with your leader, finding where the gap between your own and the team’s expectations lies and making targeted changes, you have a chance to return to the right position.

Naturally, another situation is that through communication you discover the result cannot be changed:

  • The team’s positioning of you is not what you want, and you are unwilling to match the team’s expectations
  • Your personal values and the team’s (leader’s) values differ greatly to begin with — in short, you are not on the same page
  • The constraints on you as an individual are too great, affecting your further development

At this point, either adjust your own mindset and settle into being marginalized — for one without desires there is no weakness — or quietly grow, wait for the right opportunity, build up strength for a later surge, and seek another place where you belong.

Conclusion

All in all, if you feel “marginalized” in the workplace, do not be anxious — in many cases it is not what you think it is. Have a good talk with your superior, find out where the crux lies, and a turning point may well be right in front of you.
